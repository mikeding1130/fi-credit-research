# Financial Institutions Credit Research

Independent research on the capital structures of banks and insurers — senior, Tier 2, AT1/LRCN, preferred, and where relevant the common equity — written from the issuer's side of the balance sheet.

## Why this exists

Most financial institution credit research is written from the outside in: an analyst reads a Pillar 3 report and reverse-engineers what the numbers imply about provisioning and capital. I have spent four years doing the work those disclosures describe — IFRS 9 staging and ECL modelling, sizing how much risk-weighted asset growth internal capital generation supports at a target CET1, and mapping repricing gaps to net interest income sensitivity — inside a regulated bank.

That is a narrow edge, but it is a real one, and it is the only thing here worth your time: I am better placed than most to say when a provisioning cycle is about to turn, and why the distance from a reported capital ratio to a binding constraint is usually not what the headline number suggests.

## Method

Every note answers five questions in order:

1. **The call.** Conclusion first, with a target level and a time horizon.
2. **Where I differ.** What the market is pricing, what I think it has wrong, and the mechanism behind the difference. A note without this section does not get published.
3. **The mechanism.** Usually one of: IFRS 9 stage migration versus the timing of provision build, the true distance from CET1 to a binding constraint, repricing gaps and their effect on internal capital generation, or the economics of an AT1 call against the reality of supervisory approval.
4. **Three key assumptions.** Each one observable, each with a date on which it can next be tested.
5. **What would make me wrong.** Specific and falsifiable. "If the macro deteriorates" is not an answer.

Positions are sized explicitly, and the sizing rationale is written down — a good idea and a large position are two different decisions.

Every closed position gets a post-mortem that asks two independent questions: was the outcome right, and was the process right? Confusing the two is how people abandon a sound method after bad luck, and keep an unsound one after good luck.

## Track record

This is a model portfolio. No client capital, no positions taken. Entries are timestamped by commit history, and the benchmark was fixed before the first note and is not revised.

The point is not the return. The point is whether the attribution shows the return came from issuer selection and capital-structure positioning rather than from market beta.
