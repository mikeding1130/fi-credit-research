## 1. The call

*Three sentences at most. The conclusion goes in the first one.*

> I think [instrument] should trade [tighter / wider] by [X] bp over the next [N] months, because [the single most important mechanism].

## 2. Where I differ from consensus

*Required. A note without this section is a summary of disclosures, not research.*

The market is pricing [X], which implies [Y]. I think that [understates / overstates] [Z], because [mechanism].

## 3. The mechanism

*One angle, developed properly — not four angles listed.*

[Candidates: the speed of IFRS 9 stage migration against the timing of provision build and release; the true distance from CET1 to the binding constraint, including two quarters of organic RWA growth; how repricing gaps transmit to net interest income and from there to internal capital generation; the economics of an AT1 call against the practical constraint of supervisory approval; whether the spread ladder across same-tier, same-rating issuers reflects the actual difference in asset quality.]

## 5. What would make me wrong

*Required. At least two, each specific enough to be observed.*

Not this: *"If the macro deteriorates, this call fails."*

This: *"If Q4 Stage 2 balances rise more than 15% quarter on quarter, or management guides cost of risk above XX bp on the earnings call, the provisioning-inflection thesis does not hold."*

- If ___ , I am wrong.
- If ___ , I am wrong.

## 6. Why this size

*One or two sentences answering why this weight and not double it — confidence in the thesis, how quickly the falsifying condition becomes observable, and correlation with what is already held.*

## 7. Sources

*Every material figure carries a citation. Where something is uncertain, say so.*

- Source: [Issuer] Pillar 3 Report, Q_ 20__ , p. __
- Source: [Issuer] Annual Report 20__ , p. __

## 8. Investment Conclusion

*Required. Written last, and never longer than the two lines below.*

> **[BUY / ADD / HOLD / TRIM / SELL / AVOID] — [Issuer] [Instrument] at [current level]. Target [level] within [N] months. Size [X]% of portfolio.**
>
> **Wrong if:** [the single most observable falsifier, and the date it can next be checked].

Section 5 lists everything that could break the thesis. This names the **one** condition that breaks it first and is easiest to observe — the one worth setting a calendar reminder for.

Every field here maps one-to-one onto a column in the portfolio ledger, so transcription is mechanical and nothing gets quietly re-decided after the fact. It is also the line that gets lifted into the monthly letter and, eventually, into a cover letter.

If it does not fit in two lines, the note does not yet have a conclusion.
